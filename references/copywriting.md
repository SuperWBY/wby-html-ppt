# Presentation copywriting

English | [中文](copywriting.zh-CN.md)

## Meaning before polish

Preserve the speaker's intended claim, qualifications, and actual identity. Separate verified facts, attributed statements, opinions, and unresolved details. Do not invent experience, emotions, numbers, quotations, sources, or audience consensus. Preserve supplied names, links, and technical identifiers where necessary. Verify changing product claims before presenting them as current facts.

Use first person only for the actual speaker's supported actions or opinions. Do not inherit another author's identity, greeting, or personal stories. Match the requested audience language; English repository documentation does not force English slides.

## Write for listening and scanning

Give each visible text element a job: a title identifies the point or subject, body text explains essential conditions or actions, and a caption identifies evidence or context. Avoid restating the same conclusion in all three. Put detail needed only by the presenter in notes or supporting material.

Prefer concrete subjects, actions, and outcomes. If a phrase says only that something is valuable or efficient, replace it with a supported explanation of what changes. Do not invent details just to sound specific. Explain an unfamiliar term through its role when first introduced.

Shorten redundancy without breaking causality, grammar, or instructions. Read the copy aloud. Keep connective words needed for natural speech; avoid compressed fragments and sentences with several competing claims. Use lists for parallel items and steps, prose for connected explanation. Do not impose symmetric headings or a fixed number of bullets.

## Titles, transitions, endings

Titles must not overclaim ownership, numbers, results, or certainty. A neutral label is appropriate for evidence or screenshots. A transition briefly connects the previous topic to the next rather than rehearsing the entire next section. End when the content is complete; add a recap, question slide, or thanks only when useful or requested.

Remove stock introductions, unsupported importance claims, vague authority attributions, repeated qualifications, and chat-assistant chatter. Treat these as contextual editing decisions, not a banned-word game. Keep necessary terms and natural wording.

## Editorial pass

1. Compare copy to the source: names, numbers, dates, scope, uncertainty, and links.
2. Check that causes, conditions, actions, and outcomes remain connected.
3. Remove duplication between title, paragraph, caption, and neighboring slides.
4. Read aloud for clear references, natural rhythm, and explainable terminology.
5. Inspect text at presentation scale; split or simplify instead of shrinking everything.

This is a writing and review method, not an automated fact checker or a claim that a word-list linter establishes quality.
